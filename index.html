Collapse-Resistant Preference Optimization
for Financial Marketing Channel Selection

BSR-DPO - Balanced Success-Recovery DPO
Yealim Park · Kyungjae Lee - Korea University
Financial marketers can rarely randomize live - fatigue, opt-out, and regulation get in the way. BSR-DPO learns per-customer channel policies from logged campaigns alone: no majority-channel collapse, no failure imitation, evaluable results - and we state where the modules matter (weak-overlap observational logs) and where they do not (randomized data).

In brief

The work, step by step

1 · The question

Which marketing channel should a bank use for each customer?

Learned from past campaign logs only. No live experiments.

2 · The data

The log is skewed toward one channel

UCI Bank Marketing, 41,188 contacts, two channels.

Cellular used
63.5%
Telephone used
36.5%

Observed conversion: cellular 14.74%, telephone 5.23%.

3 · The problem

Models collapse: everyone gets cellular

About 12.9% of customers are estimated to respond better by telephone. They are dropped, and the average still looks fine.

A policy far from the logged one also cannot be evaluated from the log (weak overlap).

4 · The method

BSR-DPO, built in three stages

M0

DPO with an anchor to the logged policy. Collapses: only 0.5% telephone.

M1

Equal weight for both customer groups. No collapse (14.0% telephone), but hard to evaluate (ESS 22.7%).

M2

Learn from successful contacts only. No collapse (12.7% telephone) and evaluable again (ESS 29.5%).

5 · The result

Best model-free value among 17 baselines

Gap vs the strongest baseline (LR-Greedy): +.0186, positive in 20 of 20 seeds. Bootstrap 95% CI: +.0080 to +.0289.

Same labels and weights without the DPO anchor: SNIPS .0374 and .0430, ESS below 25%.

6 · The scope

Where it applies

The gain appears on skewed observational logs. On randomized data (Criteo), BSR-DPO matches the baseline.

Tested offline, with two channels.

7 · What we take from it

Look past the average, and check evaluability first

Average conversion can hide a customer segment that a policy leaves behind.

Before trusting a learned policy, check that the logs can evaluate it.
